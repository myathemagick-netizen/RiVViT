# Nobel entries: recognition is not the underlying work

Research note and intake design correction, 2026-10-28.

The annual Wikipedia entry groups Robert Hofstadter and Rudolf Mössbauer under the 1961 Physics Nobel. That does not identify one joint discovery, one laboratory, one day, or a point of novelty. Preserve the original listing and use it as a research lead.

## Separate occurrences

| Occurrence | Time | Place and evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Award recognition | 1961 in the imported listing | Do not invent a day/location from this listing alone. |
| Ceremony | 10 December 1961, if recording the presentation | Stockholm Concert Hall for the Stockholm prizes; the Peace Prize is a separate Oslo ceremony. Venue recurrence is context, not proof that a particular year's ceremony took place without exceptions. |
| Hofstadter's research | Work underway in the early 1950s; exact breakthrough day not established here | Stanford's High Energy Physics Laboratory/Mark III accelerator, supported by Stanford's institutional history and an experimental paper. |
| Specific research publication | 15 November 1953 | Hofstadter, Fechter and McIntyre's paper describes the Stanford apparatus; the publication date is not an asserted date of experimental insight. |
| Mössbauer's underlying work | Requires its own research record | TUM identifies his thesis work and 1957 discovery at the Max Planck institute in Heidelberg. Do not reuse Hofstadter's Stanford location for both recipients. |

## Sources checked

- [Original 1953 experimental paper](https://journals.aps.org/pr/abstract/10.1103/PhysRev.92.978): publication date 15 November 1953; the paper reports electron scattering using Stanford's linear accelerator. Its received date, 26 June 1953, is a separate documentary milestone.
- [Stanford Physics: Robert Hofstadter](https://physics.stanford.edu/people/robert-hofstadter): arrival at Stanford in 1950, research using Mark III and the body of work recognized in 1961.
- [Stanford Historical Society oral history](https://historicalsociety.stanford.edu/robert-hofstadter): direct route to interviews about those early experiments, more specific than the faculty-interview index.
- [The venue's Nobel ceremony history](https://www.konserthuset.se/en/about-us/our-operation/a-venue-for-award-ceremonies/the-nobel-prize-award-ceremony): recurring 10 December presentations at Konserthuset since 1926, with historical exceptions.
- [Hofstadter's banquet speech](https://www.nobelprize.org/prizes/physics/1961/hofstadter/speech/): identifies Stockholm, 10 December 1961. A banquet is not the award presentation itself.
- [Official Peace Prize venue history](https://www.nobelpeaceprize.org/nobel-peace-prize/about-the-nobel-peace-prize/nobel-peace-prize-celebrations/award-ceremony?instance=0): Oslo, with the University Aula used in 1947–1989 rather than today's City Hall.
- [TUM biography of Mössbauer](https://av.ph.nat.tum.de/Bios/Moessbauer.php): places the thesis work and 1957 finding at Heidelberg. Further documentation is needed for finer experimental chronology.

The user-supplied Nobel facts pages were blocked by the web reader during this check. Their award-time affiliation can be retained as a lead, but the working Stanford location above is supported independently by the original experimental paper and institutional account. Nothing establishes an assumption that prize-winning work always happened in the preceding year; the 1953 paper predates the award by eight years.

## Place labels and coordinates

For a ceremony record a useful human label is **Stockholm Concert Hall (Konserthuset), Stockholm, Sweden**. For a research record use the institution/laboratory supported by its own sources. A label describes what place the event record concerns; it is not a coordinate syntax.

The user's city coordinates `59°19′46″N 18°4′7″E` convert to latitude `59.329444…`, longitude `18.068611…`; GeoJSON orders these as `[18.068611…, 59.329444…]`. They locate the city reference point supplied by Wikipedia, not a verified concert-hall location. Conversion does not improve spatial accuracy or identify the historical laboratory.

The workbench now accepts pasted DMS coordinates, GeoHack links and decimal latitude/longitude pairs. Numeric/region controls remain under Advanced. It adds an event role, research progress, unresolved questions and supporting source links. Imported awards start as recognition records needing research. They remain out of approved exports until the research state is explicitly resolved; the original year/date and text are not silently replaced.

These changes make uncertainty recordable, not automatically solved. Linking separate child events and automated biographical/paper research are the next collection workflow; this update does not claim to have found an exact discovery day or geocoded every institution.
